The Present

A father and daughter make an ordinary journey through a landscape structured by waiting, checkpoints, and unequal mobility.

Director
Farah Nabulsi

Checkpoint coverage

A day shaped by waiting

Farah Nabulsi's The Present compresses the spatial logic of occupation into a single day: a father and daughter attempt to cross Checkpoint 300 to buy an anniversary gift. The film opens with Yusef sleeping on cardboard to secure his place in line, an image that captures carceral time before a word of dialogue has been spoken.

Framing and spatial refusal

Nabulsi's visual composition draws on the politics of verticality. Overhead shots render the crowd of waiting men as a managed mass. Low-angle shots follow boys scaling the bars to cut the line, small acts of spatial refusal that the camera treats with quiet attention.
